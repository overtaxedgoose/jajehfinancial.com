Real Estate

Brokerages, investors, developers and property managers: property-level P&Ls, commission structures and investor reporting across every entity in the portfolio.

Book a Discovery Call

Real estate is the one industry where the business and the wealth are the same asset. The accounting has to work at three levels at once, each property, each entity and the whole portfolio, and it has to be clean on the day a lender, a partner or a buyer asks to see it.

That means an entity-per-property structure that stays separate in the books and not just on paper, intercompany balances that reconcile, capital accounts that are current, and a tax position built around depreciation, exchanges and basis rather than discovered at filing time. From a brokerage’s commission splits to a developer’s draw schedule, it is all covered.

What the Books Have to Handle

Property- and Entity-Level Books

A P&L and balance sheet for every property and every LLC, consolidated into one owner view. Intercompany loans and due-to and due-from balances reconciled, so the liability protection you set up is reflected in the records a court or a lender would read.

NOI, Capex & Reserves

Net operating income separated from debt service and owner items, a written capitalization policy for repairs versus improvements, and reserves funded per door so a roof does not become a capital call.

Brokerage Commissions

Splits, caps, team structures, referral fees, commission advances and agent 1099s, with revenue recognized at closing and earnest money and escrow handled under state rules. Company dollar is the number that matters, not gross commission income.

Development & Construction

Job costing by phase, hard and soft costs, draw schedules, retainage and lien waivers, change orders, capitalized interest, and budget to actual against the pro forma, so a project’s margin is known before the last draw.

Investor & Partnership Reporting

Capital accounts, preferred returns and waterfall calculations, capital calls and distributions, and K-1s delivered on time. Investors who get clear quarterly reporting fund the next deal.

Tax Strategy

Depreciation schedules, cost segregation and bonus depreciation, 1031 exchanges, passive-activity and real estate professional rules, and basis tracking, planned with our CPA firm before the transaction rather than after it.

The Numbers That Drive Profitability

The numbers lenders, partners and buyers will ask for, by property and for the portfolio as a whole. If you own the asset, you should see them before they do.

Net Operating Income

Rental income minus operating expenses, before debt service. The value of an income property is a multiple of this number, so every dollar of NOI is worth many dollars of equity.

Tracked by property, every month

Debt Service Coverage

NOI divided by annual debt service. Lenders write covenants here; you should see a breach coming quarters ahead.

1.25x or better

Cash-on-Cash Return

Annual pre-tax cash flow divided by cash invested, by property and for the portfolio. The honest answer to “is this still a good deal?”

Measured against your hurdle rate

Economic Vacancy

Rent lost to vacancy, concessions and bad debt as a share of gross potential rent. Physical occupancy hides what concessions cost.

Known by property, trending with the market

Company Dollar (Brokerages)

Gross commission income minus agent splits: what the brokerage actually keeps. Overhead has to be managed against this number, not GCI.

Margins are thin; manage overhead to company dollar

Margin on Cost (Development)

Projected profit divided by total project cost, updated with every budget revision and change order.

15 to 20% or better, contingency intact
