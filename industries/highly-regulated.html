Highly Regulated Industries

State-licensed operators in cultivation, processing and retail, where the accounting rules are strict, the tax code is unforgiving, and the books get inspected.

Book a Discovery Call

In a state-licensed market the books are part of the license. Regulators can inspect them, the state tracking system has to reconcile to them, and federal tax law treats the business unlike any other. Most of what passes for bookkeeping elsewhere is a compliance failure here.

The disciplines are specific and non-negotiable: Section 280E, which disallows ordinary deductions for plant-touching businesses and makes cost of goods sold the whole tax conversation; seed-to-sale inventory that has to tie to the state tracking system; cash-heavy operations that call for controls most companies never need; and entity structures that keep the licensed business, the real estate and the brands separate. We run these books for operators who need them done quietly and done right.

What the Books Have to Handle

Section 280E & Cost Accounting

For as long as Section 280E applies to plant-touching entities, what counts as cost of goods sold decides the tax bill. Inventory costing under Section 471, a documented allocation methodology, and records that survive an examination.

Seed-to-Sale Reconciliation

Inventory in the books has to tie to the state tracking system, by batch and by month. Shrinkage, waste and destruction logged and reconciled, because an unexplained variance is a license problem before it is an accounting problem.

Cash Controls

Cash-intensive operations need dual custody, daily counts, armored pickup schedules and banking relationships that understand the sector. Every dollar traced from the register to the deposit.

Entity Structure

The licensed operating company, the real estate, the intellectual property and the management company kept separate, with intercompany agreements and management fees that hold up to scrutiny.

State & Local Compliance

Excise and sales taxes at several levels, license renewals, reporting deadlines and required financial disclosures, calendared and met. Compliance is a monthly discipline, not an annual scramble.

Audit & Investor Readiness

Books that satisfy regulators, sector lenders and investors, with the documentation that makes a quality-of-earnings review or a license transfer possible when the time comes.
