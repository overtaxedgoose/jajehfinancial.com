Multi-Entity Companies

Holding companies, private-equity platforms and roll-ups: consolidated financials, clean intercompany, and reporting that works at every level of the structure.

Book a Discovery Call

A group of companies is not a company. Each entity has its own books, bank accounts, payroll and tax return, and the owners need to see all of them as one business without losing the ability to see each one alone. Consolidation is where most multi-entity groups quietly lose control.

The work is a chart of accounts that is the same everywhere, intercompany transactions that eliminate cleanly, management fees and shared costs allocated by a written policy, and a close calendar that produces entity-level and consolidated statements by the same date every month. For private-equity platforms and roll-ups, add acquisition integration: opening balance sheets, purchase accounting, and a reporting package that investors and lenders accept.

What the Books Have to Handle

Uniform Chart of Accounts

One chart of accounts and one close calendar across every entity, so a number means the same thing everywhere and consolidation is arithmetic rather than archaeology.

Intercompany Eliminations

Loans, management fees, shared payroll and transfers recorded on both sides, reconciled monthly and eliminated in consolidation. Due-to and due-from balances that tie, every month.

Cost Allocations & Management Fees

Shared services, executives and overhead allocated by a documented policy that satisfies partners, lenders and the tax return, and that survives a change of ownership.

Acquisition Integration

Opening balance sheets, purchase price allocation, earn-out tracking, and the first ninety days of bringing an acquired company onto the group’s systems and close calendar.

Multi-Level Reporting

Entity P&Ls for operators, consolidated statements for the board, covenant reporting for lenders and the investor package, all produced from one set of books.

Tax Structure

Partnerships, S corporations and C corporations inside one group, K-1s and allocations, state nexus across entities, and planning with our CPA firm before the structure gets ahead of the accounting.
