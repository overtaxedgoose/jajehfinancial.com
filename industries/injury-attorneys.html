Injury Attorneys

Trust accounting, case cost tracking and settlement distribution for contingency-fee firms, with zero tolerance for error.

Book a Discovery Call

A personal injury practice is a portfolio of investments. The firm advances the costs, carries the payroll and waits months or years for a fee that arrives all at once, or not at all. The accounting has to manage that portfolio while keeping client trust funds flawless, because a trust shortfall is a bar complaint even when it is an honest mistake.

The firms that scale treat the case inventory like the asset it is: valued, aged and forecast, with marketing spend tied to signed cases and every advanced dollar tracked to the file. That is what lets you hire the next attorney, borrow on your own terms and take the bigger case with confidence.

What the Books Have to Handle

IOLTA & Trust Accounting

Client funds separate from operating funds, a ledger for every client, no negative balances, and a three-way reconciliation of bank, book and client ledgers every month, documented to the standard your state bar expects.

Advanced Case Costs

Filing fees, medical records, experts and depositions are advanced for the client, tracked by case, recovered at settlement and written off on the losses. For most contingency firms they are loans to the client rather than deductible expenses until the case resolves.

Settlement Distribution

Fee calculation, cost reimbursement, medical and Medicare liens, co-counsel and referral splits, and the net to the client, laid out on a settlement statement and disbursed from trust in the right order with the right 1099 reporting.

Case Inventory & Cash Forecasting

Expected fee, stage and age for every open matter, rolled into a probability-weighted forecast. Contingency revenue is lumpy; the forecast is what keeps payroll, rent and litigation funding calm between settlements.

Marketing & Intake Economics

Television, digital and referral spend tracked to signed cases, cost per signed case by channel and intake conversion rates, so the marketing budget is a return calculation rather than a leap of faith.

Compensation & Fee Splits

Attorney origination and resolution bonuses, co-counsel agreements, referral fees, paralegal cost allocation and 1099s for experts, all reconciled to the fees actually collected.

The Numbers That Drive Profitability

The gauges that tell you whether the firm is a business or a series of fortunate settlements. Most firms know their biggest fee of the year; fewer know these.

Average Fee per Resolved Case

Total fees collected divided by cases resolved, tracked by case type and referral source. Case mix explains most changes; the trend tells you whether intake is improving.

Rising, with case mix explained

Cost per Signed Case

Marketing and intake spend divided by new signed cases, by channel. The number that decides next year’s advertising budget.

Known for every channel

Sign-to-Settle Cycle

Median days from signed retainer to disbursed fee. Every extra month is more cash tied up in the case inventory.

Measured, and shortened where the case allows

Case Cost Recovery

Advanced costs recovered at resolution divided by advanced costs incurred. Leakage here is pure lost profit.

90% or better

Months of Operating Cash

Operating cash divided by monthly overhead. Lumpy fees demand a bigger cushion than most businesses carry.

6 months or more, or a committed line of credit

Trust Reconciliation

Three-way reconciliation completed, reviewed and signed off by a partner.

Every month, zero exceptions
